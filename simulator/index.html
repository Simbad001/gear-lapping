Transmission error spectrum

Mesh frequency

Teeth engage at pinion teeth × shaft speed. Errors that repeat on every tooth show up as tall lines at 1×, 2× and 3× this frequency. These lines are the whine, and lapping lowers them most.

Sidebands

Runout and eccentricity make the mesh force rise and fall once per revolution. That modulation puts sidebands on each side of the mesh lines, spaced at the shaft frequency. Condition monitoring watches them grow as gears wear.

Tooth-to-tooth errors

Uneven tooth spacing differs from tooth to tooth, so it repeats once per revolution. It spreads energy over many shaft orders and raises the floor between the mesh lines. Lapped pairs show a lower, cleaner floor.

Simplified model, drawn as a spur pair. Spiral bevel and hypoid sets mesh at the same tooth frequency. Transmission error is the sum of the mesh harmonics, a fixed pattern of tooth spacing errors on each gear, and runout that modulates the mesh once per revolution.
